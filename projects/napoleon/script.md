## Full voiceover script — verbatim (191 words)
Napoleon was offered peace and a crown. So why didn't he just stop? In 1812, he marched about 600,000 men into Russia. Barely 100,000 came back, and his army was broken. In October 1813, at Leipzig, the Battle of the Nations, the Allies beat him again. That November came the Frankfurt proposals. He could remain Emperor, and France would keep its natural frontiers, the Rhine, the Alps and the Pyrenees, including Belgium. He would give up Poland, Spain, and most of Italy and Germany. Metternich warned that these were the best terms he would ever get. Napoleon stalled, expecting to win the war. On December 2, his foreign minister finally said yes. Too late. The offer had been withdrawn. In early 1814, at the Congress of Châtillon, the Allies demanded more: France back to its 1791 borders, without Belgium. Napoleon refused. On March 31, 1814, the Allies took Paris. In April, Napoleon abdicated and was exiled to Elba. He returned in 1815, lost at Waterloo on June 18, and died in exile on St. Helena on May 5, 1821. Each time he waited for a better deal, the deal got worse.

## Task 2 research — key points Claude flagged
Every date and number supplied checks out against sources, with these nuances:
1. Paris fell in March, not April — Allies entered Paris 31 March 1814; abdication came in April (conditional 4 April, unconditional 6 April, treaty 11 April); the script says "On March 31, 1814, the Allies took Paris. In April, Napoleon abdicated."
2. For the first offer Claude used "stalled, expecting to win" (matching the brief's "delayed") rather than "refused"; for Châtillon he used "refused" as specified.
3. The good deal was shakier than it sounds — Britain objected to France keeping Belgium; the script presents it as an Allied offer without overselling its firmness.
4. 1791 (not 1792) for the Châtillon demand, as specified.
5. Caulaincourt's assent dated 2 December per Britannica.
6. 1812 army estimates run 450,000–650,000 crossing the Niemen; survivors about 93,000–120,000, only about 35,000 of them French; the script says "about 600,000 men," not "Frenchmen."
