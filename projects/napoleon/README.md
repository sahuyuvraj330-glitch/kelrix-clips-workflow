## Description

Napoleon was offered peace and his crown in November 1813 — France could keep its natural frontiers, including Belgium. He stalled, expecting to win. By the time his foreign minister said yes on December 2, the offer was gone. At Châtillon the Allies demanded France back to its 1791 borders. He refused — and lost Paris, his throne, and finally everything at Waterloo.

## The story in 60 seconds

In 1812 Napoleon marches about 600,000 men into Russia; barely 100,000 come back. At Leipzig in October 1813 the Allies beat him again. That November comes the Frankfurt offer: remain Emperor, keep the natural frontiers. He stalls. On December 2 his foreign minister finally says yes — too late, the offer is withdrawn. At Châtillon the terms are harsher: France back to its 1791 borders. He refuses, loses Paris, abdicates, returns for a hundred days, and loses everything at Waterloo. Each time he waited for a better deal, the deal got worse.

## Facts checked

- Frankfurt proposals, November 1813: remain Emperor, keep natural frontiers including Belgium; withdrawn by December; Caulaincourt assented December 2, too late.
- Congress of Châtillon, February–March 1814: France back to 1791 borders, without Belgium; refused.
- Paris fell March 31, 1814; abdication April 1814; exile to Elba; Hundred Days; Waterloo June 18, 1815; died on St. Helena, May 5, 1821.
- Russia 1812: about 600,000 marched in, barely 100,000 came back (estimates vary — kept approximate).
