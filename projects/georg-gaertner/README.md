## Description

After the war ended in 1945, German prisoner Georg Gaertner escaped from Camp Deming, New Mexico — and hid in plain sight in America for 40 years as Dennis Whiles. Of 2,222 German prisoners who tried to escape American camps, he was the only one never recaptured. He surrendered on September 11, 1985, became a U.S. citizen in 2009, and died in 2013, aged 92.

## The story in 60 seconds

The war is over — so why does a German prisoner escape? Gaertner crawls under the gates of Camp Deming in September 1945, catches a westbound freight train to California, and becomes Dennis Whiles: ski instructor, husband, American. His hometown is under Soviet control, so going home means a camp in Siberia. The Army closes his file in 1976. Forty years after he escaped, he walks into an immigration office and surrenders — "Hitler's last soldier."

## Facts checked

- Escaped September 1945, Camp Deming, New Mexico; westbound freight train to California.
- 2,222 German prisoners tried to escape American camps (Smithsonian); he was the only one never recaptured.
- Surrendered September 11, 1985, San Pedro, California. No charges filed.
- U.S. citizen in 2009; died 2013, aged 92.

Sources: Smithsonian; AP; Los Angeles Times.
