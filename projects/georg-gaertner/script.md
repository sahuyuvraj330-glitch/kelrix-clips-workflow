## Voiceover (194 words, ~67 seconds)

The war was over. So why did a German prisoner escape? His name was Georg Gaertner, an Afrika Korps sergeant captured in Tunis in 1943 and held in America. In September 1945, at Camp Deming, New Mexico, he crawled under the gates and jumped a westbound freight train to California. He wasn't trying to get home. His hometown, Schweidnitz, was now under Soviet control, and he feared being sent back. So he became Dennis Whiles, with a Social Security card in that name. He worked as a ski instructor, a tennis teacher and a construction salesman, and in 1964 he married Jean Clarke. Of 2,222 German prisoners who tried to escape American camps, he was the only one never recaptured. His face was on FBI wanted posters, yet in 1976 the bureau canceled his warrant. On September 11, 1985, he surrendered to U.S. immigration officials in San Pedro, California. Headlines called him "Hitler's last soldier." No charges were filed. He became a U.S. citizen in 2009, and died in 2013, aged 92. For forty years he hid in plain sight, and for the last nine of them, the FBI had already closed his file.

## Claude's flags (for the record)

- "Hitler's last soldier" is both the LA Times' 11 Sept 1985 headline AND the title of his co-authored book (Hitler's Last Soldier in America, Stein & Day). Script says "Headlines called him…" so it never claims the LA Times coined it.
- Escape date: AP quotes his account as 21 Sept; Wikipedia says 22 Sept. Script says "September 1945."
- "Weeks after the war ended" holds only against Japan's formal surrender 2 Sept 1945 (Germany surrendered in May). Script says "The war was over."
- "FBI hunted him until 1963": Wikipedia credits the Army with a manhunt to 1963; script avoids the claim, uses 1947 posters + 1976 cancellation.
- 2,222: Smithsonian says 2,222 prisoners tried to escape; HistoryNet says 2,222 escape attempts; AP/NYT say "about 2,000." Script uses 2,222 as specified.
- Rank: AP says "Sgt."; NYT says "sergeant-trainee." Script uses "sergeant."
- Citizenship in 2009 comes from secondary sources only (Wikipedia, WarHistoryOnline) — easy to cut.
- Surrender was staged for publicity (lawyers arranged it, book timed to it). Script says only "surrendered to U.S. immigration officials."
- Unused extras: earlier alias "Peter Petersen"; 1952 rescue of train passengers trapped by an avalanche (NYT).
